PLC (EtherNet/IP)

The ClearCore is also an EtherNet/IP adapter: a PLC reads the machine's state every few milliseconds and, when it takes control, enables, starts, stops, triggers and jogs it, and sets its speed, timeline override and cut length. The profile itself still comes from this app (Upload); the PLC runs it.

Pick DINT for the data and the sizes are 40 (input) and 16 (output): the variables then match the maps below as they are. With SINT they are 160 and 64 bytes; copy the input into a DINT[40] and a DINT[16] into the output each scan with COP. The configuration variable comes out larger than set: it carries nothing, leave it.

Connection timeout: uploading a profile pauses the board's EtherNet/IP traffic about 50 ms (the flash is being written). Keep the timeout (RPI × multiplier) at 80 ms or more: 20 ms × 4, or 10 ms × 8. With 10 ms × 4 the PLC would see the connection drop during an upload.

Wrong sizes show as a module fault 16#0127 (output size) or 16#0128 (input size): check the sizes above. 16#0106: another PLC (or tool) already owns the connection.

Who is in control

The PLC is in control while its connection is open, it is in Run and it holds PLC_CONTROL (ControlFlags bit 1). The board strip then shows PLC … in control with an amber light and greys out Upload, Forget, Enable, Simulate, Start, Trigger, Hand jog and the cut length: the board refuses them. Stop, Abort and Disable always work, from the app or the PLC. Without PLC_CONTROL a PLC only reads, and can still stop, abort and drop ENABLE. Releasing PLC_CONTROL hands the running machine back to the app without stopping it.

  • Edges (START, STOP, ABORT, CLEAR_FAULT, TRIGGER) act when a bit turns on. Hold a bit for at least one RPI. A bit already on when the connection opens, or when the PLC goes to Run, is not an edge: nothing starts by itself after a reconnect. Turn it off and on again.
  • Levels: ENABLE on enables, off disables. SIMULATE on is sim 1, off sim 0. JOG on is hand mode (below).
  • Nothing to acknowledge. A refused edge or setpoint changes nothing and shows its reason in RejectCode; the next accepted action clears it.

When the connection drops

If the connection closes, times out (a pulled cable) or the PLC goes to Program while the PLC is in control, the board makes a controlled stop (a driven crank and the timeline ramp down at once, as a second Stop), lets the motors settle (at most half a second), then disables them and latches FaultCode 4 (COMM_LOSS). CLEAR_FAULT, then ENABLE and START, run it again.

Encoder master: the board can't ramp a line or a shaft it doesn't drive, so its followers let go at once while the line may keep moving. The PLC's own connection-loss handling must stop the line (on a flying saw, the line and the saw).

  • Setpoints apply when they change, and the non-zero ones again when the PLC takes control. Setting CrankRpm or TimelineOverride back to 0 returns to the project's value. A cut length applies from the next cut (at once with the crank off).
  • Jog: first copy where the master is into the targets (CrankRev × 360000 + CrankAngle; TimelineTime), then turn JOG on, then move the targets. The master goes there at the project's speed and ramp, every axis following its profile.
  • Trigger timing: TRIGGER reaches the timeline about 1 ms after the packet arrives, plus the PLC's RPI and scan. Where the trigger timing matters, use a wired trigger input (200 µs).
  • Waits: a timeline wait on a PLC bit passes when WaitBits has that bit at the level it wants, seen in the next packet: the RPI plus the PLC's scan, 20 ms or more. Without a connection in Run it never passes. TimelineState 6, StatusFlags bit 14, WaitIndex and WaitedMs say which wait holds and for how long, so the PLC can raise its own alarm for a wait with no timeout.

Network settings from EtherNet/IP tools

The board's address, DHCP / static and host name can be set from any EtherNet/IP tool (the TCP/IP Interface object), as well as with set on the console. A new address takes effect after the board restarts by itself; the host name at once. Both are refused while the motors are enabled or anything moves. Identity Reset type 1 puts the network back to DHCP (192.168.0.110 when no DHCP server answers).
